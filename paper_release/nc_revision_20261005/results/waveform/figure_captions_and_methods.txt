Calculation methods and scope

The supplied hourly data span 2 May 2025 to 1 May 2026. All 104 countries have 8,760 demand observations. The station basis contains 461 hourly wind and solar profiles. Demand is the retained human-volume traffic proxy; its units are arbitrary normalized request activity, with no conversion to MW or metered facility electricity. In the numerical solver, each country demand is rescaled to unit annual mean, and every domestic station profile has unit mean. This common positive scaling preserves the fitted shape and annual uncovered share. Receiver profiles are the unweighted mean of the receiver country's unit-mean domestic station columns, consistent with the submitted waveform workflow. Eligible receivers use the submitted hyperscaler-country list and RTT = 2(distance_km/200 + 20) times 1.4. The fixed-shortfall comparison uses 500 ms and full routing eligibility.

First-stage NNLS minimizes squared mismatch and rescales its fitted domestic portfolio to the original demand energy. Its non-negative residual r=max(d-domestic_fit,0) is then frozen. NNLS routing similarly fits partner profiles and rescales the resulting portfolio to the energy of r. Hourly coverage is min(r,partner_fit). The same-shortfall LP instead minimizes the sum of non-negative u, subject to u >= r - P w, w >= 0 and sum(P w)=sum(r). It is solved for the same r and P. The previous complete LP sequence additionally replaces the first-stage domestic fit; consequently, its shortfall differs and the two complete sequences need not be ordered country by country.

For numerical conditioning the routing LP uses r divided by its mean, with total energy T=8,760; the unit-energy mathematical form divides this target and every partner profile by T. Its primal weights w remain unchanged, while uncovered slack is ubar=u/T. The normalized deficit rho=sum(ubar) is computed for each country. The original annual residual is U=(R/E)rho at full eligibility. With partial eligibility the general relation is U=L/E+(R/E)rho, where L is the ineligible residual energy. Medians of these country-specific quantities must be taken after applying the identity; products of separate medians have no corresponding identity.

The stored hourly separating vector is h=-lambda, where lambda is the LP inequality dual. Its constraints imply 0<=h<=1 and equality-dual y<=-max_i h dot sbar_i. At optimum, h dot rbar + y agrees with the primal objective, and the separating margin h dot rbar-max_i h dot sbar_i agrees with rho. The archive records both the complete primal/dual arrays and explicit h, normalized rbar, normalized partner sbar, w and ubar. Hourly raw d and r are also stored in their original retained traffic-proxy units. SciPy HiGHS is configured with primal and dual feasibility tolerances 1e-8 and IPM optimality tolerance 1e-9. Independent unit-energy certificate verification uses an absolute tolerance of 1e-8 and reports actual numerical errors. The unnormalized domestic-plus-routing summary includes a maximum reduced-cost/stationarity residual of 1.2235432e-8, which is distinct from the unit-energy routing certificate. Its normalized routing checks are approximately 1e-14 or smaller.

The domestic refit fixes the already attributed hourly routing coverage C before setting remaining demand d_rem=d-C. One refit rescales the new domestic NNLS fit to original annual domestic energy E; a second rescales it to sum(d_rem). They respectively retain and reduce the domestic annual supply allocation. Since NNLS optimizes squared mismatch and subsequently rescales, neither refit is constrained to minimize the reported uncovered metric. Neither participates in the submitted Fig. 2 procedure or the fixed-shortfall certificate.

The working-hours scenario uses exp[-(h-13)^2/(2 times 3^2)] on the 24 assigned local-hour bins, normalized to mean one. This is a stipulated daily working-hours profile and does not identify a universal facility, weekday or weekend schedule. The consumer profile is the 24-bin BurstGPT token share, normalized to mean one and assigned to the same local-hour bins as an assumption. For business fraction alpha in {0,0.25,0.50,0.75,1}, the 2030 demand is 0.56 d + 0.308[(1-alpha)consumer+alpha business]+0.132, followed by rescaling to its original annual total. The fixed training schedule is retained for this comparison. All five mixes are calculated for 104 countries at full eligibility/500-ms reach. The six illustrative countries US, GB, DE, CN, IN and AU additionally receive the complete six-by-six routing grid. The original no-reshaping Fig. 2 grid is separately rebuilt for all 104 countries and all 36 combinations.

All 1,248 retained total-volume cache files were inspected for their actual timestamps, normalization and metadata. They have MIN0_MAX normalization, are non-overlapping within each country, cover 8,760 hours, and expose no retained absolute normalization factor. The metadata-derived boundaries are supplied as normalization_windows.csv. Window j receives factor 1+a cos[2pi(j-p)/12] under the conditional amplitude/offset scenarios, and the complete annual curve is then rescaled to fixed total energy. Equal-window means instead remove differences among within-window mean indices. These scenarios explore dependence on missing relative scales; none restores measured historical seasonality.

Fig. 3's covered fractions are means across countries of C/E. The corresponding identity is mean(C/E)=mean(U_dom)-mean(U_routed). Fig. 2 reports separate medians, whose difference is not the mean covered fraction. At 100, 150, 200 and 500 ms, mean covered fractions are 13.966864%, 19.008994%, 22.342743% and 26.586716%. The mean domestic residual is 39.691898%; the corresponding mean routed residuals are 25.725034%, 20.682904%, 17.349155% and 13.105182%. The published map/chord edge files omit attribution below 0.0015 of sender annual demand, while the below-row summary includes all hourly covered energy. The supplied full-grid and attribution scripts preserve those conventions and reproduce the frozen country grid, edge weights and followsun statistics to floating-point precision.
