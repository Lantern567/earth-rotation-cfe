# Strict-reliability storage calculation and deadline sensitivity

## Computational stages and denominators

Let d(t) be the 2030 Mix demand scenario constructed from the country traffic proxy, with inference share 0.308 and training share 0.132. The initial domestic portfolio is fitted by equal-energy NNLS using the country's wind and solar technology profiles. Its residual is r0(t) = max[d(t) − f0(t), 0]. At routing eligibility φ, the eligible residual is rφ(t) = min[φd(t), r0(t)]. The reachable partner matrix contains one national mean-one shape per eligible receiving country, obtained by averaging that country's available wind and solar technology columns after the technology-specific equal-energy scaling. A static nonnegative partner fit is scaled to the energy of rφ, and the covered residual is c(t) = min[rφ(t), fpartner(t)]. The remaining domestic load is deff(t) = d(t) − c(t).

The reported geometric uncovered_share is sum[r0(t) − c(t)] / sum[d(t)]. This quantity is evaluated before the cost-stage domestic refit. For storage costs, the domestic wind and solar portfolio is refitted by equal-energy NNLS to deff. Both deff and the refitted supply are then multiplied by 100 / mean(deff), yielding mean-100-MW domestic residual systems for each scenario. Generation nameplate and annual generation/ancillary costs follow the submitted front-half computation without alteration. Consequently, the Fig. 5 cost is a normalized domestic firming cost after routing, and its geometric U column does not incorporate the second fit. Receiver electricity procurement, receiver hardware and shared receiver power budgets are outside this accounting.

## Hourly service constraint

The dispatch model jointly chooses generation overbuild within [1,5], Li and LDES power/energy capacities, charge/discharge, curtailment and hourly SOC. Li and LDES round-trip efficiencies are 0.86 and 0.60; their SOC charging/discharging factors are the square roots of those efficiencies. Annual SOC is cyclic for the primary results. For every primary hourly balance, unserved(t) is fixed to zero by its variable bounds. A finite VOLL therefore cannot substitute for a zero-unserved constraint. For relaxed reliability targets ρ < 1, the explicit annual energy constraint is sum[unserved(t)] ≤ (1 − ρ)sum[deff(t)], with residual shedding priced at the original VOLL of USD 10,000/MWh. The cost denominator is the actual energy of the normalized input load, which is 876,000 MWh apart from float32 roundoff retained from the submitted calculation.

All three Fig. 5 input tables are recomputed: the six-year domestic/global table (1,248 rows), the 2030 6 × 6 routing grid (3,744 rows), and the six-year φ = 1 latency sweep (3,744 rows). Their union has 7,384 distinct country/year/RTT/eligibility keys. Exact equality of demand/supply arrays and technology costs, verified by SHA-256, permits 4,591 distinct dispatch solves; identical inputs are reused without approximate interpolation. The outputs retain each original table row and add realized unserved energy, service fraction, solver status and constraint errors. Every solve is an actual LP optimization. Neighboring cases may start from a previous optimal simplex basis through the official HiGHS interface; this changes the starting basis and leaves the model, objective and stopping conditions unchanged. Primal equality residuals, inequality/bound violations and the zero-unserved constraint are checked explicitly.

## Deadline-bounded domestic training and batch scenarios

For the additional 2030 domestic sensitivity, the unchanged Mix arrivals contain a flat training component of 0.132 and a flat batch-inference component of 0.25 × 0.308. Their combined deferrable energy fraction is f = 0.209. This sensitivity treats those components as divisible, interruptible jobs with a constant hourly arrival-energy rate a = f mean(d). It is a scheduling scenario using the real annual demand/supply profiles and the existing workload-share priors; it does not infer job deadlines, scheduler capacity or power curves from operator telemetry.

Let q(t) be executed batch energy during hour t and B(t) be end-of-hour outstanding batch energy. The constraints are B(t) = B(t−1) + a − q(t), 0 ≤ q(t) ≤ 2a and 0 ≤ B(t) ≤ aD, with cyclic annual B. For constant arrivals executed in FIFO order, the latter backlog bound implements maximum additional waiting time D at the hourly resolution. Deadlines D = 0, 1, 6 and 24 h form nested feasible sets. D = 0 forces q(t) = a and exactly recovers the fixed flat component. Cyclic conservation gives sum[q(t)] = 8760a; temporal scheduling adds no work or annual job energy. The fixed interactive component is d(t) − a, and the dispatch hourly energy balance serves [d(t) − a + q(t)] with zero unserved energy. The demand/supply portfolio and procurement parameters are held fixed across the deadline sweep, and the storage dispatch and overbuild are reoptimized jointly with q and B.

The execution cap 2a is an explicit capacity scenario, corresponding to at most 41.8 MW of batch execution in the mean-100-MW normalization. Job energy per unit work is held constant; no processor or facility idle-power saving is credited. The sensitivity excludes acquisition costs for the assumed compute headroom and does not model gang scheduling, checkpoint overhead or state transfer for non-preemptible training jobs. Hardware-dependent power, service constraints and receiver budgets are addressed separately in the operational case. The outputs report annual batch-energy conservation error, maximal backlog, realized service and numerical constraint errors. The optimized annual cost must be nonincreasing as D widens, although individual Li/LDES capacities can trade off and need not decrease monotonically.

## Window-scale uncertainty and firming sensitivity

The storage sensitivity uses the same twelve actual request-download windows and scale factors as the waveform sensitivity. For each country, the published human-traffic proxy is multiplied within window j by m(j) = 1 + A cos(2πj/12), with A = 0.25 or 0.50 and j = 0,...,11. The final partial window is weighted according to its actual 576 hours; all other windows contain 744 hours. The perturbed hourly proxy is then rescaled to its original annual sum. These factors are explicit assumptions about unidentified relative window volumes, shared across countries at the stated phase zero; they do not recover measured seasonal demand. The storage cases comprise US, DE, PS, IN, BR and AU, both domestic and global routing tiers, and three scale scenarios (published equal scales, assumed amplitude 25%, assumed amplitude 50%), giving 36 country/scenario/tier records.

After applying a factor to the original traffic proxy, the existing 2030 Mix operator, country-by-technology routing and post-routing domestic refit are executed again. The remaining domestic load and supply are independently normalized to mean 100 MW in every case. Generation/storage cost parameters, routing eligibility and annual SOC assumptions remain the same. Every dispatch imposes zero hourly unmet energy. This tests uncertainty propagation from the traffic window weights into the conditional domestic firming cost and storage capacities, and does not estimate whole-fleet operating cost or receiver-side energy procurement.

Across the six cases, amplitude-25% assumptions change total domestic firming cost by −12.49% to +14.60% and global-tier conditional domestic cost by −14.37% to +17.32%, relative to each country's published-scale case. At amplitude 50%, the corresponding ranges are −15.22% to +29.99% and −13.93% to +35.48%. Storage capacities exhibit technology substitution; global-tier LDES energy changes range from −68.76% to +60.43% at amplitude 50%. Annual scale uncertainty is therefore material for firming-cost and capacity estimates even when the equal-energy waveform residual remains positive. These are six-country, phase-zero conditional sensitivity ranges and do not imply a 104-country statistical uncertainty interval.
